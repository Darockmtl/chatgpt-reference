# INSIGHT — Provider vs Co-Creator
*2026-03-27*
*Source: Sunil Malhotra — Design Thinking Workshop for Sustainable Change (Event Design Collective podcast with Ruud Janssen)*

---

## The Situation That Produced This

Sunil is naming what is wrong with how most facilitators, designers, and speakers think about their value. He calls it the Western model. He says it plainly: I own the value proposition. I am the provider. They are the takers.

Then he names what that produces. We will continue to remain designers who are actually like doctors — providing prescriptions rather than making sure the beneficiaries themselves can actually create and co-create something.

The prescription metaphor is the clearest version of the problem.

---

## The Prescription Trap

A doctor has expertise. A doctor has authority. The patient comes in, describes symptoms, receives a diagnosis and a treatment plan. The patient leaves. They cannot replicate what the doctor did. They are dependent on the doctor to come back and dose them again. Nothing transferred except the prescription.

This is the model most professional development, most workshops, most keynote speaking, most MC work operates inside. The expert arrives. The room receives. The room leaves inspired, informed, or entertained. Six weeks later nothing has changed, because nothing was designed to transfer. The expert owned the value proposition. The room was the taker.

The alternative Sunil describes — and the drum circle demonstrates — is co-creation. The facilitator does not own the output. The output belongs to the system that produced it. The facilitator's job is to design conditions under which the room can produce something it could not have produced alone. And when it does, the room owns it. It does not need the facilitator to come back.

---

## What This Means for Anthony Horng's Work

The MC who shows up with a runsheet is providing a prescription. Here is the sequence. Here is the pacing. Here is what happens next. The room follows the prescription for the duration of the event. The event ends. Nothing grew.

The MC who enters the system and works from inside it is doing something closer to co-creation. The structure is there — Discover → Design → Deliver is not improvisation. But the structure serves what the room needs to produce, not what the MC planned to deliver. The distinction is in where ownership of the outcome sits. Does the room leave with something that is theirs? Or do they leave having received what the MC delivered?

Twenty years of holding rooms across wildly different industries produced a specific conviction: the events that mattered were the ones where the room left with something they made. Not something they received. The MC who makes that possible is not the provider. They are the condition.

---

## The Harder Version of This

Sunil says: we need to shift from provider to part of the larger system. That shift is not comfortable. Being the provider is a clear role with clear value. You own the proposition. You deliver it. You get paid for it. The outcome is yours because you designed it.

Entering the system and co-creating the outcome means releasing ownership of the result. You designed the conditions. What came out belongs to the room. Your value is not in the outcome — it is in the capacity to create conditions that produce one.

That is a harder thing to sell. It is also a harder thing to be. And it is what separates the MC with a runsheet from the one who can hold any room in any industry because the capacity lives in them, not in the plan.
