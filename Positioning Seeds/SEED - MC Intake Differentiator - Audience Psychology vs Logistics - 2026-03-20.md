# SEED — MC Intake Differentiator: Audience Psychology vs Logistics

## Raw Thought

Most MCs ask logistical and surface questions: how many people, what is the event about, what do you want your MC to do, describe your audience.

Anthony goes deeper. The intake question that separates the offering:

*Do you want a polished MC who entertains the crowd and makes the event run smooth — or do you want someone who understands what the audience is feeling and thinking so the event actually moves them?*

The distinction is not demographics. It is psychology. Not who is in the room, but what they are carrying into it, what they want to believe is possible, and what would have to happen for them to leave changed rather than just satisfied.

This becomes a differentiator in the positioning and proposal stage because it reframes the MC role before the client even knows that reframe was available.

---

## The Contradiction to Hold

Some clients just want smooth and polished. That is a legitimate need and Anthony serves it. The question is not an elimination filter — it is a sorting mechanism. It separates clients who want execution from clients who want transformation. Both are real. The offer has to be honest about both without positioning one as lesser.

The risk: framing the second option as additive rather than corrective. Some clients will read the question as a challenge to their past choices if it is not handled with care.

---

## What Makes This a Differentiator

Other MCs ask questions that are purely logistical or surface-level. Anthony's intake goes to audience psychology — not demographics, but internal state. What are they feeling walking in? What do they want permission to believe? What shift would make them leave differently than they arrived?

This positions the MC role as diagnosis first, execution second. Most MCs position around execution only. That is a category shift, not a feature upgrade.
