# STORY — The Sand Castle (Preparation)
*March 14, 2026*

Everyone thinks the beach club is the high-end crazy parties. The open bars, the DJ sets, the VIP sections. That was one part of it.

I was working the other part. Summer camps. Corporate events. The quiet hours when the club belonged to a different kind of crowd.

One morning I'm walking through the main alley — the one that cuts straight through the property, the path everyone uses to get from one end to the other. And there is a six-year-old kid, right in the middle of it. Not at the beach. Not off to the side. Dead center of the corridor, sitting in the dirt, building a sand castle.

Fully concentrated. Completely in his own world. The kind of focus adults pay thousands of dollars to learn how to access.

And people walked around him.

Not because they had to. The alley was wide enough. They could have walked straight through. But something about that kid made them redirect. He had already claimed the space. Not loudly. Not aggressively. He had just decided, with everything in him, that this was where he was building, and that what he was building mattered.

Then he started building a wall.

A small perimeter around his work. Packed sand, a few rocks, whatever he could find. A boundary between his castle and the foot traffic. It was not big enough to physically stop anyone. But nobody crossed it.

It was not the wall that kept people out. It was him. The energy of someone guarding something. You could feel it a mile away. That sense that this — right here, right now — is the most important thing in the world, and I am protecting it with everything I have.

He was six years old. And he understood something most adults have completely lost.

When something matters, you protect it. Not just with physical barriers. You have to become the protector. In your mind. In your body. In the way you hold the space around what you are building.

As adults we stop thinking that way. Too many things pulling at once. Too many interruptions that feel legitimate. Too many other people's urgencies dressed up as our own. We scatter. We let the foot traffic walk straight through the middle of what we are building because we forgot to put up the wall — and forgot to become the wall.

Building this course, I needed that kid's attitude. The distractions were not always external. Half of them lived in my own head. A better idea. A more urgent task. A question about pricing that felt important right now. Every one of them a foot landing on the castle before the walls were dry.

The structure I had to build was not just a workflow. It was a perimeter. A decision that this — right here, what I am working on at this step — is the most important thing. Not forever. Just right now.

Guard the castle. Everything else walks around you.
