# Theatre Training as the Origin of the Integrated System

## The Raw Capture

Anthony studied theatre performance at Concordia University — did not complete the degree, but trained long enough to internalize what mattered. What the training gave him was not a credential. It was the first time all the parts were taught as one integrated system: body, voice, presence, intention, audience relationship, and the moment of performance — all at once, inseparable.

Every other training domain he has worked in since — MC work, public speaking education, facilitation, corporate hosting, working alongside DJs in club environments — drew from that foundation automatically. Not consciously. Not by reaching back and applying a technique. It transferred the way real learning transfers: invisibly, below the surface, available when needed without being summoned.

Anthony's own framing: "one of the trainings I used as cross experience in all other stages of my life and work."

---

## Why This Matters

Most public speaking training teaches parts in isolation. Voice coaches teach voice. Presentation coaches teach slides. Confidence programs teach mindset. Each targets a component. None teaches the integrated act.

Theatre taught the whole system at once — because theatre has no choice. You cannot perform with presence but no voice. You cannot carry intention without a body that delivers it. You cannot respond to an audience you are not watching. The parts only make sense in relation to each other, under live conditions, in real time.

This is the same logic that makes Discover — Design — Deliver work as a framework. It is not three separate skills bolted together. It is one integrated act — clarity feeds structure, structure feeds execution, execution serves the audience. That logic did not come from a curriculum committee. It came from theatre training.

Design thinking gave the framework its architecture. Theatre gave it its body.
