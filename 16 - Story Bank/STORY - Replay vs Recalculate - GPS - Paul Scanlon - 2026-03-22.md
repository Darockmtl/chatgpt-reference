## The Illustration — Full Version

Paul Scanlon was using his GPS. He took a couple of wrong turns. Missed a couple of basics.

The GPS never mentioned it. Didn't change its tone. Didn't say: if you'd listened to me, you would have got there by now. Didn't remind him of all the exits and turns he'd missed in the last five years. No language of guilt, recrimination, blame, or shame.

It simply recalculated.

On screen: rerouting. Or: recalculating. What it did not do was replay. It did not replay the mistakes. It just recalculated the journey.

His question: are you a replay or a recalculator?

You get to choose. You can dwell on the exit you missed. You can dwell on the wrong turns. That is replaying.

Or you can accept the mistake, recalculate, and reroute — because GPS gives you new destination options. There are many ways to get to the same end. That is true in GPS. It is true in life.

---

## Why This Works as a Stage Illustration

The GPS is a shared experience. Every person in the room has been rerouted. The illustration meets them exactly where they are — a familiar, mundane moment — and lifts it into a principle about how people respond to their own mistakes. No preamble needed. No explanation of the setup. The image is already loaded in the audience before Scanlon says a word.

The emotional payload arrives in the contrast: the GPS has no shame response. The human does. That gap is where the insight lives.

The closing question — replay or recalculator — is binary and personal. It asks the audience to locate themselves in the frame immediately. That is what a good illustration does. It does not explain a principle. It makes the audience feel where they stand in relation to it.

## Teaching Applications

**Deliver module — recovery as a learnable skill:** Use after establishing that mistakes are inevitable on stage. The GPS illustration reframes recovery not as resilience or personality, but as a mechanical choice. Recalculating is what the GPS does by default. For speakers, it requires deliberate practice — because the human default is replay.

**Workshop exercise prompt:** Ask participants to identify one moment in a recent talk or presentation where they went into replay mode. What triggered it? How long did the loop run? What would recalculating have looked like in that moment?

**Design module — structure as a recalculation map:** When you have a well-designed talk, you always know where you are in the architecture. Recalculating is fast because you have a map. When you are improvising the whole time, a stumble is catastrophic because there is no map to return to. The GPS works because it has the full route loaded. The speaker who designed their talk has the same advantage.

## Stage / Keynote Use Notes

Strong enough to use as a full signature moment with explicit attribution. Deliver the illustration in full, in Scanlon's voice (paraphrased, credited). Then make the pivot to the speaker's world:

"The GPS does not replay. It recalculates. Every time, automatically, without shame. That is not what most of us do when we stumble in a room. We replay. And the talk that was recoverable becomes unrecoverable — not because of the stumble, but because of the loop we ran after it."
