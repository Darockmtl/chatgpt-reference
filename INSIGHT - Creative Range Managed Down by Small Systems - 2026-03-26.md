# INSIGHT — Creative Range Managed Down by Small Systems
*2026-03-26*

---

## The Peterson Frame

Peterson's core claim: hierarchies need creative people at the top, but they filter them out at the bottom. The system is designed to train compliance, not generate ideas. Creative people are only valuable once there's enough latitude — and by then, most of them have been squeezed out or left.

The sharper claim: you can't evaluate a creative person within an evaluative structure without destroying the very thing you're measuring. If they're operating inside the system's rubric, they're not being creative. They're being assessed.

---

## What This Reframes for Me

The 20+ years of stage work wasn't failing to climb a ladder. It was building lateral range that systems couldn't generate.

The bottom of the hierarchy — the part that runs on compliance, standardization, and legibility — had no place for me. Being unassessable is not a flaw. It's a feature of the profile.

---

## The Subtler Version Peterson Doesn't Name

He talks about creative people being filtered out. The subtler version is staying in but being quietly edited. The ideas that landed after they went through someone else's mouth. The presence that bothered their order just by being present. Output extracted but the source managed.

That's not failure. That's friction between range and smallness.

---

## The Creativity Layer

It wasn't just being different. It was proposing movement — and movement threatened the container they'd built their identity around.

Small circle thinking isn't just limited. It's often defended. People inside it aren't unaware — they're invested. When you walk in with a wider frame and say "here's how we could grow," what they hear underneath it is: *what you've been doing isn't enough.*

They weren't just tolerating a different personality. They were managing someone who kept pointing at the horizon when they were focused on holding the ground.

---

## The Real Tension Going Forward

Peterson says the creative person builds their own structure. That's what the Academy, the brand, and the offer ecosystem are. But building your own structure still requires structure — funnels, content calendars, onboarding flows.

The question isn't whether I'm inside or outside a system. It's whether the systems I build serve the creative work or start to replace the creative with the operational.

That's a live tension. Not a resolved one.

---

## The Unanswered Question

Peterson says creative people are hard to nurse within any system. What he doesn't address: what does it cost to nurse yourself?

Building your own structure, funding your own development, staying outside long enough to actually create something — that's not just an act of courage. It's expensive. Emotionally, financially, relationally.

That's the part that doesn't make it into the clip.
