# INSIGHT — You Don't Know Our Field
*2026-03-27*
*Source: Sunil Malhotra — Design Thinking Workshop for Sustainable Change (Event Design Collective podcast with Ruud Janssen)*

---

## The Situation That Produced This

Sunil Malhotra walks into a room of senior executives at an agri-tech company. He knows nothing about agri-tech. He does not hide it. He announces it as the first substantive thing he says to the room — I am the most ignorant person here, and that is exactly why I am useful to you.

The objection that lives underneath that moment is the same one Anthony Horng encounters in client conversations and prospect meetings. It arrives in different forms. You don't know our industry. You've never worked in our sector. How can you hold a room of our people if you don't understand what we do?

The objection sounds like a credibility challenge. It is actually a description of the value being offered — stated by someone who does not yet understand what the value is.

---

## What the Objection Is Really Saying

When a client says you don't know our field, they are operating from the assumption that the MC or facilitator's job is to demonstrate domain knowledge. That the authority in the room comes from knowing more about their industry than they do. That credibility requires expertise in their specific context.

That assumption is wrong — and it is worth understanding why it is wrong before trying to answer it.

The people in that room already know their field. They are surrounded by people who know their field. Every meeting, every conference, every internal session they have ever attended has been full of people who know their field. And they are still stuck on whatever problem brought them to the event. The domain knowledge is already in the room. It is not what is missing.

What is missing is the outside frame. The question nobody inside the problem can ask because they have been living inside it too long to see it. The capacity to hear what is actually being said in the room rather than what the industry expects to be said. That is what walks in when Anthony Horng walks in. Not domain expertise. The absence of it — and everything that absence makes possible.

---

## The Mechanism

The domain expert facilitator or MC arrives performing. They have read the industry materials. They have prepared the domain-specific examples. They are managing the appearance of knowing. Senior people feel that performance within minutes. It produces a specific dynamic — polite engagement on the surface, skepticism underneath. The room gives them what they prepared for and nothing more.

The facilitator who does not know the domain and does not pretend to arrives without the performance. There is nothing to manage. All of their attention is available for what is actually happening in the room — the energy, the subtext, the question underneath the question being asked out loud. They can ask the obvious question because they have not been trained by the industry to stop asking it. They can name what nobody in the room is naming because they are not inside the system that made it unnameable.

The ignorance is not a liability being overcome. It is the mechanism that makes the value possible.

---

## How This Lives in Anthony Horng's Work

Twenty years of MC work across 3D metrology, MMA, renewable energy, women's obstacle racing, health technology, financial services. Never the domain expert. Always the one holding the room.

What that built is not domain knowledge across industries. It is the calibration to enter any room, read it accurately from the first minutes, and hold the process regardless of what the content is. The domain shifts every engagement. The capacity to be inside whatever domain you are in does not.

When a client pushes back with you don't know our field — that is the moment to name what is actually being offered. Not domain expertise. The outside frame. The capacity to hear their room the way someone inside it never could. The question nobody who knows their field would think to ask.

That is not a workaround for a weakness. It is the thing itself.

---

## The Live Tension

This needs to be said without arrogance and without apology. Both are wrong. Apologizing for not knowing the domain concedes the objection and accepts the wrong frame. Dismissing the concern sounds defensive and loses the client.

The move is to name the mechanism clearly and let the client decide whether that is what they need. Some clients need a domain expert. That is a different hire. The client who needs someone to enter their system and surface what is not being seen — that client needs the outside frame. Naming the distinction plainly is what lets the right client recognize themselves.
