# REFERENCE — Positioning Principles — Anthony Horng

## The seven principles

### 1. The audience is the hero — not Anthony

The MC's job points toward the public, not toward the performer. Copy that centers Anthony ("the bilingual MC who...", "Anthony brings...", "with 20 years of experience...") fails this principle when used alone. Copy that points toward what the audience receives, experiences, or becomes passes the test.

This is not modesty. It is the structural truth of the work. The MC who treats the event as their stage loses the room. The MC who treats the room as the subject holds it.

**Source:**
- `MY TRUTHS` — "The double loss" — the audience is also a stakeholder, communication is the delivery mechanism for value that already exists
- `MANIFESTO` — "The room only cares if it feels you care for them"
- `MY TRUTHS — 2026-03-22` — "It is not what you want to say. It is what your audience needs to hear"
- [[ARCHIVE - Anthony Horng Personal History Raw - 2026-03-27]] — "The constraint was the education. Because I was never the subject matter expert, communication was the only tool available."

---

### 2. Communication is design, not delivery

The work is understanding what another person needs to receive — not just what you want to say — and building the conditions for that to happen. The speaker is not the subject. The audience is.

Most MCs sell delivery (showing up on the day and running the runsheet). Anthony's differentiator is design (entering the system before the event, understanding the stakes, co-creating the structure that makes the message land). Copy that treats Anthony as a performer fails this principle. Copy that treats him as a partner in the design of the event passes it.

**Source:**
- `MY TRUTHS — 2026-03-22` — "Most people think about communication as delivery. That is the wrong frame. Communication is design."
- `MANIFESTO` — "You become the message" — meaning transfers through connection, not polish
- [[ARCHIVE - Anthony Horng Personal History Raw - 2026-03-27]] — pandemic study of event design with Ruud Janssen and Simon Sinek; "thinking about the whole experience as a designed system"

---

### 3. Connecter avec les humains

The core conviction stated in Anthony's own words. The promise of every event Anthony works on is connection — between the people in the room, between the room and the message, between the audience and what matters to them. Not entertainment. Not performance. Connection.

The phrase belongs in any French-facing positioning. It does not need elaboration or qualification. It carries the philosophy in three words.

The connection works on two levels at once, both essential:
- The audience connects to the message, to the moment, to what's happening on stage (the event becomes meaningful)
- The MC connects to the room — speaking to people, not in front of them

Without the second, the first does not happen.

**Source:**
- Anthony's own working language: "Le but c'est de connecter avec le public en retravaillant les textes pour que ça résonne avec eux, de connecter avec le public et j'essaie de découvrir ce qui est important pour eux"
- `MANIFESTO` — "The message is not the content. The message is the connection."
- `THE PHILOSOPHY` — speaking fluency is a byproduct of identity change, not skill accumulation

### 4. The parcours is the method, not the past

The diversity of contexts Anthony has worked in — boxing/MMA rings, weddings, clubs until 3am, obstacle races, festivals, bars, corporate galas, conferences — is not a CV liability to be hidden in favor of the corporate work. It is the proof that communication is universal, and the source of the craft. Every context taught something the corporate room would never have taught alone.

This principle is sourced verbatim from Anthony's Facebook post (2024-08-14):

> I work in the corporate world today, and when I started I was advised to not talk about the other experiences not related to the corporate world for fear of having a bad image.
>
> Guess what they are wrong.
>
> The expertise and experience on stage today is because of my old school days at bars, festivals, parties, weddings and the ring.
>
> Being a Boxing/MMA Ring announcer in a bloody ring with thousands screaming taught me focus and clarity.
>
> Being an MC for weddings I discovered how to be emotional and the value of celebrating rituals and connection and family.
>
> Working until 3 am as MC in clubs keeping up the stamina and resiliency and discipline in a chaotic and tempting environment.
>
> Motivator at obstacles races showed me that passion, determination and grit is how you get things done.
>
> Every experience, stumble and success was a learning that makes me a better speaker, communicator, and teacher.

— Anthony Horng, Facebook post, 2024-08-14

**What this means for positioning:**
The corporate MC who only ever did corporate work has a thinner toolkit. The diversity is the asset. Copy that hides or downplays the non-corporate stages fails this principle. Copy that frames each context as a source of a specific capability passes it.

### 5. Design thinking + stage craft — a new combination, not old-school

Anthony is not an old-school entertainer adapting awkwardly to corporate work. He is not a new-school consultant performing the role of MC. He is a combination — and the combination itself is the distinctive positioning.

The design thinking side: understanding what the audience needs, the structure of the experience, the architecture of the event, the co-creation of meaning before the event. This is the work most MCs don't do.

The stage craft side: 20 years of physical presence, vocal control, time control, room reading, recovery from chaos, emotional range, bilingual fluency. This is the work most consultants and designers can't do.

The combination is what makes Anthony hard to classify — and that's the point. Copy that places him in either camp alone (just an MC, or just a strategist) fails this principle. Copy that holds the tension between the two passes it.

**Source:**
- Anthony's own framing in conversation, 2026-05-18: "It's a new way of thinking. It's not old school. It's using design thinking and stage work to complement everything."
- `THE PHILOSOPHY` — communication is design, identity transformation through craft
- [[ARCHIVE - Anthony Horng Personal History Raw - 2026-03-27]] — pandemic study (Ruud Janssen / Event Design Collective / Simon Sinek) combined with 20 years of stage work

### 6. The performer vs. the actor — attention is where the work happens

The mask exercise at Concordia is the founding moment of this principle. Lying on the floor with a mask on, the task was to stand and feel the first ray of the sun. The face couldn't move — the body had to carry it. Anthony failed it. The teacher saw it through the mask: "You're forcing your eyebrows. You have to feel it — not think it." The body was tensed because the mind was watching the performance.

The beginner next to him — no clubs, no stage time, no experience — was doing the exercise. Internalizing it. The difference wasn't skill. It was where attention was. The beginner was in the experience. Anthony was watching himself have it.

That distinction — the performer who watches himself versus the actor who lives in it — is the foundation of how Anthony approaches the work. The room reads which one is present before it hears a word. Most MCs are performers in this sense — managing their own image while running the show. Anthony's approach is the actor's stance: attention outward, on the room, not on the self.

**What this means for positioning:**
Copy that frames Anthony as a polished performer fails this principle — because polish is the performer's mode. Copy that frames him as someone who is *in* the event with the audience passes it. The word *real* matters more than the word *professional* here.

**Source:**
- [[RAW - Transcript - Deliver Origin Story - Anthony Horng - 2026-03-27]] — full mask exercise transcript
- [[ARCHIVE - Anthony Horng Personal History Raw - 2026-03-27]] — Theatre Training (Concordia) section
- `MANIFESTO` — "The room does not care how polished you are. It cares whether you are real."
- `MY TRUTHS` — "Live the moment fully. The suspension of disbelief is a choice you make first."

### 7. The outsider who holds the room — communication is the credential

Across 20+ years of MC work, Anthony has never been the subject matter expert in the room. Not in MMA. Not in MUDGIRL. Not in Skills Canada. Not in 3D metrology, renewable energy, banking, health technology, or any of the dozens of industries he has hosted for.

This is not a gap in his profile. It is the proof of the work. The communication gap between Anthony and the subject matter was total in every one of those rooms — and irrelevant. Because the credential was never the topic. The credential was the communication. He held those rooms anyway.

This is what makes Anthony defensible against any objection of the form *"but he doesn't know our industry."* He has never known the industry. He has always held the room. That pattern across 20+ years is the argument.

**What this means for positioning:**
Copy that tries to position Anthony as an expert in any vertical (tech, healthcare, finance, education) fails this principle — because that's not the claim. Copy that positions him as someone whose only credential is communication, and whose proof is 20+ years of doing exactly that successfully across industries that share nothing, passes the test.

**Source:**
- [[ARCHIVE - Anthony Horng Personal History Raw - 2026-03-27]] — MC Career section: MMA, MUDGIRL, Skills Canada, Canada Day, Salon Pepsi, Miss Quebec/Canada, Beach Club, virtual events, weddings as celebrant
- [[STORY - The Outsider Who Holds the Room - Contextual Authority and MC Career - 2026-03-21]]
- Anthony's own framing: "The through-line across 20+ years of MC work is not the industry. It is the position."

## How to apply this file

**Before producing any positioning copy:**

1. Read this file.
2. Ask: does the proposed copy point toward the audience, or toward Anthony? If toward Anthony, rewrite.
3. Ask: does the proposed copy treat Anthony as a performer, or as a designer of the experience? If performer, rewrite.
4. Ask: does the proposed copy carry the spirit of "connecter avec les humains," or is it a marketing abstraction? If abstraction, rewrite.
5. Ask: does the proposed copy honor the parcours, or hide it? If hiding it, rewrite.
6. Ask: does the proposed copy hold the tension between design thinking and stage craft, or collapse into one camp? If collapsed, rewrite.
7. Ask: does the proposed copy frame Anthony as a polished performer, or as someone who is *in* the event with the audience? If polished performer, rewrite.
8. Ask: does the proposed copy claim industry expertise Anthony doesn't have? If yes, rewrite — the credential is communication, not vertical knowledge.

**For French copy specifically:**
- Never translate from English. Think in French from the start.
- The phrase "connecter avec les humains" is Anthony's own — preserve it verbatim.
- Quebec professional French register. No Québécois familiarity. No pompous tone.

**For any claim about Anthony in copy:**
- Trace the claim back to one of the seven principles above, or it doesn't belong.
- No invented framing. No "polish" claims. No competitive comparison.
- Vault-sourced facts, numbers, and client quotes only.
