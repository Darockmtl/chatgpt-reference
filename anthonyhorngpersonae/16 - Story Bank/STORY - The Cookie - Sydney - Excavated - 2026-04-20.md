# The Cookie

When Sydney was two or three, dessert was never urgent. You'd put something in front of him and he'd wander off. Come back. Take a bite. Leave again. Come back. The cookie or the cake or whatever it was just sat there, waiting, and he'd return to it on his own time like it wasn't going anywhere.

Everyone around him would say the same thing. Hurry up. Eat. What are you doing. It's good. Eat it.

He'd eat it. Eventually. Because he wanted to, not because anyone told him to.

One night I decided to teach him something. He was taking his time with dessert again, doing his thing, wandering, and I reached over and took a bite out of it. Right in front of him. The lesson was supposed to be: if you don't take what's yours, someone else will.

He couldn't say it properly — he was two, maybe three — but he was mad. And he was sad. Not tantrum sad. Quiet sad. Like something had been broken that he didn't have the words for.

I told myself it was a lesson. I was teaching him how the world works.

---

Five years later. He's nine. He and his friend are on the couch, playing on the iPad, and I give them each a cookie. Freshly baked.

His friend — I don't want to judge, but maybe he hadn't grown up with the same abundance — grabbed the cookie and ate the whole thing in seconds. Loved it. Demolished it. Looked over at Sydney's cookie, half eaten, still sitting there.

"You're gonna have the rest?"

Sydney looked at him.

"Yes."

That was it. He didn't explain. Didn't justify. Didn't say why. Just the word. Yes.

The friend had already finished. Sydney still had half a cookie. And neither of them was wrong — they were just sitting in the same room with completely different relationships to the thing in front of them.

---

I stood there watching and the bite came back. The one I took five years ago. The lesson I thought I was teaching.

He wasn't slow. He was never slow. The cookie was there and it was his and it wasn't going anywhere and he could enjoy it whenever he wanted. The value was never in the eating. It was in the fact that it was there. Available. His. Not scarce. Not urgent. Not something someone could take from him if he didn't move fast enough.

Except that one time when someone did.
