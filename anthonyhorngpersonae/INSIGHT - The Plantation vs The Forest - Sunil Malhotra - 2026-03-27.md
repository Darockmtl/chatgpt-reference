# INSIGHT — The Plantation vs The Forest
*2026-03-27*
*Source: Sunil Malhotra — Design Thinking Workshop for Sustainable Change (Event Design Collective podcast with Ruud Janssen)*

---

## The Situation That Produced This

Near the end of the conversation, Sunil is making a point about the difference between two philosophies of design. He has been talking about entering the system rather than managing it from outside, about the drum circle, about co-creation. Then he reaches for an image that contains all of it.

The difference between farming — creating a plantation — and allowing a forest to grow.

He says it simply. In one you have control and design. In the other you are an observer who partakes of what gets created because you are also nature. The plantation requires you. The forest does not.

---

## The Image Fully Opened

A plantation is controlled. High yield per acre. Predictable output. Scalable. Every variable is managed — the rows, the irrigation, the harvest schedule. And it collapses without the farmer. Remove the management infrastructure and it is gone within a season.

A forest is inefficient by plantation standards. Uneven. Dense in places, sparse in others. The output is unpredictable. But it is self-sustaining. It produces its own conditions for continuation. It does not require you to maintain it. And it produces things no farmer would have thought to plant.

The plantation designer stands outside the system and manages inputs to control outputs. The forest grows because the designer entered the system — because they understood themselves as part of what they were growing, not the manager of it from the outside.

---

## What This Is About for Anthony Horng

Most event professionals — MCs, facilitators, even speakers — work plantation logic. They arrive with a plan. They manage the inputs. They produce the designed output. The event ends. Nothing grows from it without them.

The rooms that stayed with people — the ones where something shifted in the air and nobody could name exactly when it happened — those rooms were not managed. They were entered. The MC was inside the system, not running it from the outside.

Discover → Design → Deliver is not plantation logic. It does not design the outcome. It designs the conditions. Discover is understanding what this room needs before the first word is spoken. Design is building the conditions under which something can emerge — not scripting what will emerge. Deliver is entering the system and moving with what the room produces.

The client who wants a plantation hires someone with a runsheet and a clean execution record. The client who wants a forest needs someone who knows how to enter a system and trust what comes out of it. Those are different clients. They are also different events.

---

## The Unanswered Question

Sunil says: when you become part of the larger system, you can flow with it rather than expect others to take something out of it while you remain outside.

The question this leaves open: how do you know when to initiate and when to follow? The forest metaphor suggests pure observation. Twenty years of MC work suggests the answer is more specific — you initiate the conditions, and then you follow what the conditions produce. The skill is in knowing which moment is which.

That is not a resolved question. It is a live one.
