# Personal Story Bank — Part 2: Narrative Assets

Use the completed **Personal Story Bank** as the only source material.

Work only from stories, fragments, and relationship threads that are already documented there.

If the story bank is incomplete, weak, or missing enough detail for a certain section, say so clearly.
Do not invent.
Do not guess.
Do not fill gaps.
Do not upgrade weak material into certainty.

## Goal

Turn the existing story bank into usable narrative assets for future writing, speaking, interviews, and content creation without losing truth, nuance, or human context.

This is not a biography.
This is not a brand rewrite.
This is not a motivational transformation of the stories.

The purpose is to identify which lived stories have the most value, tension, transferability, and future usefulness.

## What to Extract

Using only what is already present in the story bank, identify:

- the strongest storylines
- the most usable story scenes
- the most meaningful emotional tensions
- the clearest identity shifts
- the most revealing relationship dynamics
- the most transferable human lessons
- the most reusable anecdotes for speaking, teaching, interviews, and content

## Rules

1. Use only material already present in the Personal Story Bank.
2. Do not invent new stories, missing details, motives, or emotional meaning.
3. Do not exaggerate the importance of a story.
4. Do not smooth contradictions if they are part of what makes the story human.
5. If an interpretation is reasonable but not fully proven, label it clearly: **Inference**
6. If the source is too weak to support an asset, label it clearly: **Unknown — requires verification**
7. Stay close to the lived material, not to modern branding language.
8. Keep the focus on real human value, not on turning everything into a polished message.
