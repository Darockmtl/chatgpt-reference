# INSIGHT — Preparation as Presence — The Practitioner Version
*2026-03-27*
*Source: Sunil Malhotra — Design Thinking Workshop for Sustainable Change (Event Design Collective podcast with Ruud Janssen)*

---

## The Situation That Produced This

Sunil Malhotra is describing the night before a high-stakes workshop. Senior executives. A domain he knows nothing about. Most facilitators in that position spend the night cramming — trying to close the knowledge gap, preparing for every question, trying to become someone they are not before they walk in.

He has a couple of wines and goes to sleep.

The image is deceptively simple. What it contains is a practitioner's relationship to preparation that takes years to develop — and that most people in the room-holding business never arrive at because the anxiety is too loud.

---

## What This Is About at the Practitioner Level

The MC or facilitator who cannot sleep the night before a big room is not being conscientious. They are being occupied by fear. The distinction matters because the behavior looks identical from the outside — late night, active mind, running through the event — but produces completely different results in the room.

Fear-based preparation fills the mind with material that needs to be managed in the room. Every scenario rehearsed becomes something to track. Every domain fact crammed becomes something to protect. You arrive with a full load and spend the event managing it. The room has to compete with your own internal process for your attention.

Presence-based preparation is different in kind, not just degree. It means doing the structural work thoroughly — understanding the arc of the event, knowing the room's purpose, being clear on what this group needs to leave with — and then putting it down. Trusting that the preparation is inside you and does not need to be held consciously in the room. Then getting the rest that makes presence possible.

The wine is not the point. The permission to stop preparing is the point. Knowing when enough is enough — when continuing to prepare is no longer building capacity and has become managing anxiety — that is a practitioner's judgment. It develops through hundreds of rooms. It cannot be taught in a briefing document.

---

## What This Has Produced Over 20 Years

The rooms that worked were not the ones with the most preparation. They were the ones where the preparation was complete enough that it disappeared — where the structure was so thoroughly internalized that there was nothing left to manage, and all of the attention was available for what the room was actually doing.

The rooms that did not work — or worked less than they should have — were often the ones with the most visible preparation. The thorough notes. The rehearsed transitions. The prepared answers to anticipated questions. All of that preparation in the room, being managed instead of the room itself.

Twenty years of MC work across wildly different industries is not twenty years of learning new domains. It is twenty years of learning to trust the preparation enough to put it down. To walk into a room in 3D metrology or MMA or women's obstacle racing without performing knowledge of those fields — and to hold the room anyway, because the capacity to hold a room does not live in the domain. It lives in the practitioner.

---

## The Unanswered Question

There is a version of this that tips into arrogance or complacency. The facilitator who does not prepare adequately and calls it presence. The MC who wings it and calls it reading the room.

The distinction is real and it matters. Sunil has his assets. He has his frameworks, his slides, his cue cards, his materials on the walls. The structural preparation is done. What he does not do is spend the night trying to become someone he is not. The permission to stop is earned by the preparation being complete, not by skipping it.

Knowing the difference — when preparation is genuinely done versus when anxiety is filling the space that rest should occupy — is the practitioner's judgment that cannot be handed to someone else. It develops in the room, not before it.
