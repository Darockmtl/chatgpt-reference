# SEED - Speaker Sheet Positioning Gaps — Problem-Solution Reframe for Four Services

**Source:** AI-generated analysis of Anthony's speaker sheet. Found in `Clippings/processed/2025-06-04.md`.

---

## What Clients Are Actually Looking For

They don't wake up saying "I need a narrative-driven host." What they want:

- Someone who will make their event feel alive, not flat
- A speaker who won't be generic motivational filler
- A partner who will help them look good in front of their boss, board, or attendees
- Confidence that the show won't fall apart if the schedule changes or energy drops
- A message that doesn't get lost in a sea of presentations

---

## Three Positioning Gaps Identified

**Gap 1: Describing services, not solving problems**

Current: *"Narrative-Driven Delivery: Keeps the show on track without feeling scripted."*

Problem: Vague. Doesn't name the actual pain.

Better: *"When events risk feeling flat or scattered, I guide the energy, link the moments, and keep your message front and center."*

Rule: Each service should say — here's the fear or need you have. Here's how I make it disappear.

---

**Gap 2: Positioning isn't tight enough**

Current: *"Speaker. Strategist. Communication designer."*

Problem: Sounds like a jack-of-all-trades to decision-makers.

Better: *"Strategic event speaker. Trusted partner for bold events. Message delivery with staying power."*

Words that imply outcomes, not just roles: strategic, trusted, message delivery.

---

**Gap 3: Biggest differentiators are buried**

60,000+ women annually, bilingual, 20+ years, national broadcast — none of it pops.

Recommendation — add a credibility bar directly under the name or intro paragraph:

> *"20+ years. 60,000+ audience members a year. Trusted by brands like Skills Canada, TEDx, and Canada Day."*

---

## Service-by-Service Reframe

### 1. Narrative-Driven Delivery

Current: *"Keeps the show on track without feeling scripted. Designed to guide attention, link segments, and lead with energy from start to finish."*

Problem: "On track" is vague. Doesn't show why it matters.

Better: *"When timing shifts or energy dips, I help the audience stay locked in and your message stay sharp — from welcome to wrap-up."*

---

### 2. Impact Speaking

Current: *"Short-form talks that act as strategic anchors in the agenda. These aren't filler keynotes — they shift energy, build connection, and land the message."*

Problem: "Short-form talks" might confuse clients who want "a speaker" not a format.

Better: *"A sharp, memorable message that shifts the room's energy and gets people leaning in — without eating 45 minutes of stage time."*

---

### 3. Script & Flow Design

Current: *"Agendas become storylines. Every transition, cue, and phrase is shaped to keep attention high and meaning clear."*

Problem: Poetic but vague. Doesn't clarify if you do this with the team or independently.

Better: *"I help shape the event's rhythm — writing transitions, scripting key segments, and making sure every speaker flows into the next with purpose."*

---

### 4. Speaker Coaching (via Speaker Academy)

Current: *"For internal speakers, execs, and teams who want more than stage presence — they want structure, confidence, and a message that sticks. Built through guided feedback and real-time practice."*

Problem: A bit long. Doesn't highlight the unique edge.

Better: *"Your internal speakers don't need another theory class — they need support that works in real time. I coach them to feel ready, sound confident, and deliver with clarity — using tools they can apply on the spot."*

---

## Design Thinker Summary (from source)

Three axes to write to:

- **Their fears:** boring events, unengaged guests, last-minute speaker issues
- **Their dreams:** a smooth show, a speaker that makes them look good, a moment guests remember
- **Their language:** results, not jargon — "message that moves people to action" not "anchored interventions"
