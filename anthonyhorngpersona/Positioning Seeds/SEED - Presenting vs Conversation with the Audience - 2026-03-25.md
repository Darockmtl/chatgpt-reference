# SEED — Presenting vs. Having a Conversation with Your Audience

**Captured:** 2026-03-25
**Source:** Boréa postmortem debrief — emerged during case study build
**Tags:** #mc-work #differentiator #positioning #facilitation #speaker-academy #content

---

## The One-Line Version

Most MCs present *at* a room. The best ones are in *conversation* with it — and that's a preparation choice, not a personality trait.

---

## The Insight

Presenting is one-directional. You move through material, hit your marks, hand off the mic. Clean. Professional. And it keeps the audience exactly where it found them — passive.

Conversation requires that you've done enough work in advance to actually *listen* live — to catch what's being said, build on it, and thread it back to the goals without losing the room.

The script becomes a starting point, not a ceiling. When a presenter says something unexpected, you catch it. You build on it. You connect it back to what the audience needs and what the organization came here to do. Nothing goes off-track, because you already defined what "on track" means — before you ever walked on stage.

That's the difference between pre-canned questions and a discussion that's actually alive.

The pre-work is what makes the live responsiveness possible. Without Discover → Design, going off-script is a risk. With it, it's just good facilitation.

People don't ask questions when they're being presented at. They ask questions when they feel like they're part of something.

---

## Live Proof

Boréa Table Ronde 2026 — audience participation was the highest in the event's history. Not because questions were planted. Because the room felt like a conversation, not a performance.

*"Tu écoutes, tu ne fais pas juste ton script, mais tu écoutes réellement ce qui se passe sur scène pour être capable après ça de ressortir les éléments. C'est un gros point de différenciateur parmi les différents animateurs qu'on a eus dans les dernières années."*
— Chanelle Turgeon Gervais, Communications, Boréa Construction
