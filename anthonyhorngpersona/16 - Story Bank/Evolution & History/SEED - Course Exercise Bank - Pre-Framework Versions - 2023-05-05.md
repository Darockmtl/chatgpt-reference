# SEED — Course Exercise Bank — Pre-Framework Versions — 2023

*Extracted from four pre-framework course outline ChatGPT sessions (Versions 1–4, May 2023).*
*These exercises predate Discover → Design → Deliver but the practice mechanics are still valid.*
*Pull when building course modules, workshop exercises, or live lab activities.*

> The module structure these came from is superseded. The exercises themselves are not. Evaluate each on its own merits when building the actual course.

---

## Nervousness and Mindset

- Deep breathing exercise before speaking
- Visualization exercise — rehearse the speech going well before delivering it
- Positive affirmations and positive self-talk practice
- Record yourself giving short speeches to build confidence and analyze progress
- Identify your specific nervous habits (pacing, fidgeting) and practice interrupting them
- Mindfulness exercises to calm the nervous system before speaking
- Empathy exercises — practice shifting focus from yourself to what the audience needs
- Audience analysis practice — anticipate questions, frame the speech around audience needs

---

## Audience and Message

- Audience analysis worksheet — demographics, values, beliefs, needs
- Practice tailoring the same message to different audience types
- Key message exercise — define the single most important thing the audience must leave with
- Practice using metaphors and analogies to make key messages concrete
- Repetition technique practice — identify where repetition reinforces the message
- Crafting a compelling opening and conclusion — practice separately, then integrate

---

## Structure and Design Thinking

- Outline practice — build speech structure before writing full content
- Transitions exercise — write and deliver only the transitions between sections
- Empathy mapping — map the audience's current state, needs, and desired state
- Brainstorming and mind mapping for speech content ideation
- Problem definition exercise — define what problem the speech solves for the audience
- Rapid prototyping — sketch a rough version of the speech, deliver it, revise
- Practice exercises for different speech types: informative, persuasive, inspirational — one speech per type

---

## Rhetoric

- Identify logos, pathos, ethos in a speech you admire — analyze how each is used
- Practice writing one argument using each of the three appeals separately
- Repetition, metaphor, and analogy application — rewrite a section of a speech using each
- Persuasive argument practice — build an argument, test it against objections

---

## Storytelling

- Story arc mapping — plot a personal story onto a standard arc structure
- Character creation exercise — describe a real person from a story in vivid detail
- Conflict and resolution practice — identify the tension and turning point in a story
- Practice telling the same story in 30 seconds, 2 minutes, and 5 minutes
- Storytelling to resonate — identify what the audience shares with the protagonist

---

## Voice

- Breathing exercises for voice control and projection
- Vocal warm-ups before speaking
- Vocal range exercises — practice speaking at different pitches intentionally
- Intonation and rhythm exercises — mark a script for emphasis, pause, and pace
- Pause and emphasis technique — deliver one passage with no pauses, then with deliberate pauses — compare
- Record and analyze vocal delivery — identify patterns in pace, volume, pitch

---

## Body Language and Stage Presence

- Posture practice — speak while standing against a wall to feel neutral alignment
- Hand position exercise — practice the five basic hand positions (open, steepled, etc.)
- Facial expression practice — deliver the same line with different facial expressions
- Eye contact drill — practice sustained eye contact with one person per thought
- Gesture and movement — identify where natural gestures reinforce meaning
- Stage movement with intention — practice moving to a new position only on a new idea
- Blocking practice — map out movement on a physical or imagined stage

---

## Engagement and Facilitation

- Opening question practice — write three different questions that could open the same speech
- Rhetorical question technique — rewrite statements as questions to increase engagement
- Suspense technique — identify where tension can be delayed before resolution
- Humor — find one moment of self-deprecation or observation that fits naturally
- Prop use — identify one physical object that could anchor a key idea
- Audience energy management — practice reading the room and adjusting pace or energy
- Interaction technique — design one moment where the audience does something (raise hand, reflect, turn to neighbor)

---

## Stage Performance and Drama

- Rehearsal routine — structured practice sequence before delivery
- Stage fright management — practice entering the stage with intention, not apology
- Use of the full stage — practice delivering to different sections of an imagined audience
- Blocking — practice moving with purpose, stopping with stillness
- Improvisation — practice responding to an unexpected question or disruption
- Pace and volume for dramatic effect — mark one passage for slow/fast and loud/quiet contrast
- Dramatic pause — practice holding silence for three full seconds before a key point

---

## Assessment mechanics (from Version 4 — could inform course build)

- Multiple choice quiz at end of each module
- Short homework assignment to apply the module technique in real life
- Commentary section for learner questions — asynchronous feedback loop
- Final exam covering all modules
- 30-minute video per module format
