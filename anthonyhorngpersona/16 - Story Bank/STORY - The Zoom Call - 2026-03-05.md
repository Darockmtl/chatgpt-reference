# The Zoom Call

Someone introduced us. She said he was her mentor. I set up the call to explore — I was curious whether an online health entrepreneur and an MC could find common ground, something neither of us had thought of yet.

He started by asking about me. My health. My hobbies. What I liked, what I didn't. It felt like genuine curiosity at first. Like he was actually interested in who was sitting across from him.

Then the pivot. Every answer I gave became an entry point. My habits, my lifestyle, my schedule — all of it fed directly into a pitch for his online health product and program. The questions weren't curiosity. They were reconnaissance.

From there it was a pitch dressed up as a conversation. Part used car lot, part Tupperware party — the online edition — delivered with the confidence of someone who had stopped noticing the room a long time ago.

I stopped him. More than once. Told him this wasn't in my priorities right now. Tried to bring it back to what I actually called for — out of the box thinking, something unexpected, a real exchange. He heard me and kept going. Eventually he offered me a spot in his affiliate program. A way for both of us to benefit, he said.

After thirty minutes I'd had enough. I said so and left the call frustrated and drained.

There was a third person on the call — the one who introduced us. She barely spoke. She was sidelined by the same current that was pulling everything toward him.

---

## What Was Actually Happening

He never asked what I needed. He asked what he could use.

The questions about my health and hobbies felt real in the moment. That's what made it worse. The interest was a setup — a way to find the angle, locate the gap, position the product. By the time I realized what was happening, I was already inside the pitch.

The energy had a specific quality to it. Not hostile. Not dishonest. Something closer to desperation with a polished surface. Borderline narcissistic in the way it kept circling back to him — his work, his impact, his program. Every opening I gave him became an on-ramp back to the same destination. You can feel that in real time. It lands before you can name it.

The sale was always the priority. Not the person across from him. Not the conversation. The sale.

I left drained not because he was difficult. I left drained because nothing real was exchanged. Being in that room cost something and gave nothing back.

---

## Teaching Potential

- The difference between genuine curiosity and curiosity as a sales tool
- What it feels like to be a prospect when you thought you were a peer
- How the illusion of interest — questions that are actually reconnaissance — is its own kind of manipulation
- The energy people start avoiding — not because you're wrong, but because being around you costs something
