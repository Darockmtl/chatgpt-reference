# STORY — The Vault and What It Contained

*Raw material. First-person. Draw from this for keynotes, course intros, positioning content, interviews.*

---

There is a folder in my Obsidian vault called Clippings.

It has somewhere over four hundred files in it. URLs, screenshots, half-formed notes, articles I bookmarked at midnight, transcripts from videos I watched twice. A record of everything I thought was worth keeping over the last few years — everything that made me stop and think: *that's it, that's something*.

I never organized it. I just kept adding to it.

This year, I decided to go through it. Not curate it — go through it. Read every file. Decide what it was worth. Extract what was real and route it somewhere it could actually be used.

It took weeks. It is still taking weeks.

What I expected to find: good material for the course. Frameworks I had collected, ideas I had half-developed, source material that could become lessons.

What I found instead: I had been building something for years without knowing I was building it.

Not a course. Not a framework. A *position*. A way of understanding what communication actually is — where it begins, why it fails, what it takes to get past the thing that stops most people before they ever open their mouth.

---

The Clippings vault is not a library. It is more like geological strata. You can see, in the dates on the files, what I was thinking about in 2023, in 2024, in 2025. The same questions kept surfacing in different languages. How do people change? Why do some speakers command a room and others disappear into it? What is the difference between someone who communicates and someone who performs communication? Is there a structure underneath all of this — or is it just talent, which is just luck, which means it can't be taught?

I believed it could be taught. I had believed that for twenty years. I just hadn't found the architecture that proved it.

---

Then one afternoon, processing a batch of rhetoric files — dry academic sources, Britannica entries, Encyclopedia.com — something surfaced.

The Five Canons of Rhetoric. Cicero. Quintilian. First century AD.

Invention. Arrangement. Style. Memory. Delivery.

I looked at them and felt something I can only describe as recognition. Not learning something new. Remembering something I already knew.

DISCOVER. DESIGN. DELIVER.

The same structure. The same logic. The same sequence of operations that any communicator has to move through — ancient Rome or present day, Senate floor or conference keynote.

I sat with that for a while.

Then I noticed something else. Something the classical system had no answer for.

Cicero was writing for men who were already standing up. Already in the courtroom. Already in the forum. His problem was how to make them more effective.

He never had to ask the question I spend most of my work answering.

*What about the person who won't stand up?*

The person who has something to say and has learned, somewhere along the way, that it is not safe to say it. The person who has been managed down — by school, by family, by ten thousand small moments where their voice was met with silence or dismissal or laughter. The person who has convinced themselves that they are not a speaker, as if that were a fixed quality rather than a layer of sediment over something that was always there.

The classical tradition assumed you were willing. I start with people who are not.

That is the work. Not making you a better speaker.

Making you a speaker.

---

The vault contained something I didn't expect: evidence that I had been circling this for years. Notes on fear. Notes on identity. Notes on the gap between knowledge and application, between knowing what to do and being able to do it in front of people. Notes on what it feels like to step onto a stage for the first time — not the mechanics of it, but the internal experience.

I had been building the DISCOVER module without knowing that's what I was doing.

The frameworks came later. The Dreyfus model. The Four Stages of Competence. Systematic desensitization. CBT for public speaking fear. All of it pointing at the same thing: the first obstacle is not skill. It is identity. It is the story a person carries about whether they are someone who speaks.

You don't fix that with technique. You fix it by getting underneath it.

That is DISCOVER. That is what the vault kept trying to tell me.

---

I do not know exactly when I will launch Speaker Academy. I know it will happen this year.

What I know now that I did not know before I opened the vault: the thing I have been building is not a public speaking course. It is a restoration project.

The speaker is already there. The course removes what is blocking them.

I wrote that sentence a long time ago, not entirely sure I believed it. I believe it now.

The vault contained hundreds of documents pointing toward that single claim. I just had to go through all of them to find it.
