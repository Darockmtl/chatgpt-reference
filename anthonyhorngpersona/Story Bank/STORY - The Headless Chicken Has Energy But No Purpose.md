# The Headless Chicken Has Energy But No Purpose

## The Sequence

Not leader versus follower. Not brave versus afraid. Not confident versus insecure.

Purpose.

The headless chicken has energy but no purpose. Leroy Jenkins charges but has no purpose. The performer goes through the motions but has no purpose.

All are moving. None know why.

The person who walks in with purpose has done the Discover work. They know who they are talking to, what they are there to say, and why it matters to someone in that room.

---

## Why It Works as a Teaching Sequence

Three characters. One pattern. One reveal.

The headless chicken — pure energy, zero direction. Running hard, going nowhere.

Leroy Jenkins — charges in without a plan, famously. The bravado is real. The intention isn't.

The performer — the most dangerous of the three. Technically present. Internally absent. The room can feel it even if they cannot name it. Something is being performed at them rather than shared with them.

All three look like they are showing up. None of them are.

The reveal is not a technique. It is not a framework step. It is a single word from a coachee when pushed on what separates the ones who hold the room from the ones who don't.

"They have purpose."

---

## Origin

This sequence emerged in a live coaching session. The coachee was working through the two kinds of vulnerability — unprotected versus protected — and was asked what the distinction actually was. Not leader versus follower. Not brave versus afraid. The answer that landed was purpose. The headless chicken sequence was the illustration that followed.

The sequence is stage-ready. Three characters, escalating recognition, single-word reveal. Works as a cold open, a course module entry point, or a diagnostic frame in a keynote.
