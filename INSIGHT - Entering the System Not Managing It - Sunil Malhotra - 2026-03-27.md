# INSIGHT — Entering the System Not Managing It
*2026-03-27*
*Source: Sunil Malhotra — Design Thinking Workshop for Sustainable Change (Event Design Collective podcast with Ruud Janssen)*

---

## The Situation That Produced This

Sunil Malhotra is describing what separates a facilitator who holds a room from one who manages a sequence. He is not talking about MC work — he is talking about design thinking workshops. But the distinction maps exactly onto the difference between what most MCs do and what Anthony Horng does.

The standard MC arrives with a runsheet. They track the clock. They execute the sequence. They announce what is next. They are adjacent to the event — professional, reliable, technically correct. The event happens and they manage the surface of it.

What Sunil describes — and what 20 years of stage work produces — is something different. You enter the system. You become part of what is happening rather than a manager of it from the outside. The room is not something you run. It is something you are inside.

---

## What Entering Means

Entering the system means your attention is on what is actually happening in the room — not on the schedule, not on the next slide, not on the planned transition. You are reading the room in real time and making decisions from inside it rather than executing a plan designed before you arrived.

This is not improvisation. The structure is there. The arc is prepared. But the structure is held loosely — as a framework that serves what the room needs, not as a script that the room serves.

The MC who manages from outside is always one step behind the room. They are tracking the plan. The room has already moved. The MC who has entered the system moves with the room because they are inside it. They feel the shift before they can name it. They make the adjustment before the room notices one was needed.

---

## What This Distinguishes

The client who hires a regular MC is hiring someone to execute a sequence reliably. That is legitimate value. It is also replaceable. Any competent MC with the same runsheet can do it.

The client who works with Anthony Horng is getting something that cannot be handed to someone else with a briefing document. The 20 years of stage work across 3D metrology, MMA, renewable energy, women's obstacle racing — that is not a credential. It is the calibration that makes it possible to enter a room you have never been in before and read it accurately in real time. The domain shifts. The capacity to be inside whatever domain you are in does not.

That is not a positioning claim. It is a description of what actually happens in the room when the MC has entered the system rather than managed it from outside.

---

## The Live Tension

Entering the system requires releasing attachment to the plan. That release only becomes possible when the structure is so thoroughly internalized that it no longer needs to be held consciously. The MC who has to think about the runsheet cannot enter the room — they are occupied. The one for whom the structure is automatic has nothing left to manage. All of their attention is available for what the room is actually doing.

This is why the Discover → Design → Deliver framework is not just a teaching tool for Speaker Academy students. It is the operating philosophy of how Anthony Horng works with any room. Discover — know what this room needs before you walk in. Design — build the conditions, not the outcome. Deliver — enter the system and move with what emerges.
