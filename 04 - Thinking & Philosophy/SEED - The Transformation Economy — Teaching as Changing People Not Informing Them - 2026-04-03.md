# SEED — The Transformation Economy

## The Core Tension

Pine & Gilmore's Experience Economy gave us the language for designing moments. Now there's a next layer: the Transformation Economy. Not "what did they experience?" but "who did they become?"

Kaospilot (same source as the 5E model) frames it this way:

> From memorable experiences to meaningful lives.

That's not marketing language. That's a design philosophy.

## What This Means for Anthony's Work

Anthony doesn't run events. He doesn't just hold rooms. If the work is actually about communication as identity — which is the philosophy that anchors everything — then the output of a good session isn't that people clapped. It's that people left with a new relationship to their voice.

That's the transformation frame.

**The question it raises for positioning:**
Is Anthony selling Speaker Academy as a course that teaches public speaking skills? Or as something that restructures how someone sees themselves as a communicator?

Those are different offers. Different prices. Different audiences.

## The Risk of Stopping at Experience

A well-designed experience can be impressive and still leave no trace. The audience feels something, but it doesn't reorganize anything. Most speaking — even excellent speaking — operates here.

The shift into transformation is riskier, slower, and less immediately legible. But it's where the work actually lives.
